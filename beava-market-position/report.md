## Executive summary

The independent real-time feature-store category collapsed in the last 12 months. Tecton (Aug 2025) and Fennel (Apr 2025) were acquired by Databricks; Featureform was acquired by Redis (Oct 2025) and relaunched as Redis Feature Form (Apr 2026). The "hyperscaler-bundled feature platform" segment did not exist 18 months ago.

Beava's strongest fits are NOT head-on with feature stores. They are: **(a)** collapsing the DIY Kafka+Flink+Redis stack into one binary (H5 Lead); **(b)** sitting underneath Apache-2.0 OSS rules engines (V12 Lead, via the Osprey gap Discord opened in July 2025); **(c)** the velocity/cost counter substrate behind LLM gateways (V11 Lead, a brand-new <2-year-old segment in active 2026 consolidation).

Beava is **not** a Flink replacement (H2 Watch), not a streaming OLAP replacement (H4 Avoid head-to-head), not an AML platform replacement (V7 Watch), and not a graph-DB replacement (X3 Watch — graph DBs are complementary). Beava cannot replicate the consortium data moats of identity (X4) or crypto-intel (V6) vendors, but can credibly be the velocity-feature backend underneath them.

### The single most reproducible gap
`validation_signals_today` was marked uncertain in 19/21 segments. Beava has zero public users, zero published case studies, zero design-partner announcements in nearly every segment — even segments where the technical fit is excellent. The fix is design-partner hunting + repo demos, not engineering.

## H1 — Real-time / online feature stores (independent)

**Use-case refs:** 18a, 18b, 10, 21

### Competitive landscape

**Incumbents.** Three independent (non-hyperscaler-bundled) feature stores define this segment as of May 2026: (1) Chalk — real-time feature platform, proprietary cloud SaaS, raised $50M Series A at $500M valuation in May 2025 (led by Felicis); sub-5ms feature retrieval; named customers Whatnot, Apartment List, Medely, Doppel, Sunrun, Socure, Found, iwoca. (2) Feast — Apache-2.0, Linux Foundation AI/Data Foundation project, Red Hat blog endorsement (May 2025), joined the PyTorch Ecosystem; online store is BYO (Redis, DynamoDB, Postgres, MySQL, Snowflake, Feast is a fetch/serving layer, not a compute layer. (3) Hopsworks — AGPL-v3 open-core, RonDB-backed online tier (sub-ms p99, SIGMOD 2024 paper claims 10x lower p99 vs SageMaker/Vertex). Tecton (acq. Databricks Aug 22 2025) and Fennel (acq. Databricks Apr 17 2025) are no longer standalone. Featureform was acquired by Redis Oct 9 2025 → "Redis Feature Form".

**OSS license posture.** Mixed — the segment's defining buyer filter. Feast is Apache-2.0 (cleanest). Hopsworks is AGPL-v3 open-core (a non-starter for most enterprise legal teams per Google's published AGPL Policy). Chalk is proprietary closed-source SaaS. Beava's Apache-2.0 is at parity with Feast and materially cleaner than Hopsworks/Chalk.

**Hyperscaler bundling threat.** Medium-to-High. Databricks (Tecton+Fennel) and Redis (Featureform) collapsed roughly half of the prior independent feature-store market into hyperscaler bundles within 6 months. SageMaker FS (DynamoDB online), Vertex AI FS (Bigtable), Databricks Online Feature Store/Lakebase are credible "good-enough" alternatives bundled into existing hyperscaler subscriptions. This is the highest-bundling-pressure segment beava competes in.

**Integration surface.** Independent feature stores split sharply on ingestion model. Feast/Hopsworks assume upstream Spark/Flink/dbt computes features and writes them to the online store on a schedule (materialise-and-push), with Kafka/Kinesis the dominant streaming-input path. Chalk owns the compute side but expects Kafka/Kinesis. Beava's HTTP/JSON :8080 + framed-TCP :8081 push-on-event model is materially differentiated — it accepts webhook-style ingestion and mobile-SDK direct push WITHOUT a Kafka broker hop. Fit for mid-market buyers without a streaming platform team (Medely/Apartment-List shape); friction for tier-1 buyers already standardised on Kafka.

**Scoring engine coupling.** Both. Independent feature stores are scorer-agnostic and feed external rules engines (Drools, FEEL, in-house DSL, Snowflake SQL, Chalk's own resolver DSL) and ML scorers (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, ONNX). Beava plays the same role.

### Beava position lens

**Position today.** demo

**Credible wedge.** (1) Single binary collapses (Kafka+Flink+Redis+Feast) or (Spark+RonDB+Hopsworks) into one process — meaningful for ML platform teams without a streaming-platform team; Feast/Tecton/Hopsworks all expect upstream Spark/Flink. (2) ~60 first-class aggregation primitives baked into the server vs Feast (explicitly a fetch-layer). (3) Sub-millisecond TCP batch_get at parity with Chalk's sub-5ms / Tecton's published 0.8ms / Hopsworks 1.93ms-avg-3ms-p99 100-row batch and materially faster than Vertex AI FS's ~30ms. (4) Push-on-event ingestion over HTTP/JSON + framed-TCP without Kafka — mobile-SDK direct push and webhook patterns work natively. (5) Apache-2.0 with no open-core upsell and no acquisition risk — structural hedge against the Databricks/Redis bundling pressure that took out Tecton/Fennel/Featureform.

**Disqualifiers.** (1) No TLS/auth on :8080/:8081/:8090 — procurement non-starter; every managed competitor ships TLS+IAM out of the box. (2) No in-process sharding — Whatnot/DoorDash/Instacart-scale state cannot fit on one box (single-tenant ceiling ~684k EPS, ~100-200 GB hot state). (3) No multi-region active-active replication — global ranking/authorisation paths require cross-region online stores. (4) No first-class offline/online parity story — beava is online-only; buyer must own offline consistency themselves. Feast/Tecton/Hopsworks/Chalk all market "point-in-time correctness" as a headline feature. (5) No materialisation-from-warehouse. (6) No multi-tenancy / namespace ACLs. (7) No SDK ergonomics for tabular ML idioms — Feast/Tecton ship `get_online_features(entity_rows=DataFrame, ...)` returning a DataFrame; beava's get/batch_get is lower-level. (8) No feature monitoring / drift detection. (9) No on-demand feature transformations at request time (Tecton's "On-Demand Feature Views", Chalk's "resolvers"). (10) No managed/hosted offering.

### Evidence and verdict

**Verdict.** **Compete** — credible play with 3-9 months of focused engineering on TLS/auth/multi-tenancy/sharding plus a published recsys-rerank and online-FS-for-fraud demo with a paired Python notebook against XGBoost. Wedge (single binary + 60 primitives + Apache-2.0 + sub-ms batch_get + no-Kafka push) is real and differentiated. Realistic play: design-partner with one mid-market ML platform team in fraud/risk (18a) or live-commerce/marketplace recsys (18b) where Tecton-now-Databricks lock-in is unappealing and Chalk's SaaS pricing is the friction. Avoid pitching tier-1 buyers (Whatnot/DoorDash-scale) until in-process sharding ships.

**Confidence.** high

## H2 — Stream processing engines

**Use-case refs:** 5, 13, 14

### Competitive landscape

**Incumbents.** Apache Flink (24k+ GitHub stars, the de-facto enterprise stateful-streaming engine — Alibaba 40B events/day, plus Netflix, Uber, Stripe, LinkedIn, DoorDash, Lyft, BMW, Apple). Apache Spark Structured Streaming with Real-Time Mode GA (sub-300 ms p99 stateful, 5 ms minimum end-to-end on Databricks Runtime 16.4+). Materialize (BSL 1.1, Postgres-compatible streaming MV database, Community Edition capped at 24 GiB RAM / 48 GiB disk, $108M raised, visible layoff/uncertain signal 2024-2025). RisingWave (Apache-2.0, Rust, ~8.8k stars, $46M raised). ksqlDB (Confluent — feature-frozen since the 2023 Immerok acquisition). Kafka Streams (JVM-embedded). 2025-2026 entrants: Arroyo (Rust, 5x sliding-window throughput vs Flink), Bytewax, Pathway, Fluvio, EventFlux. Beava is NOT a stream-processing engine — it covers the count/mean/quantile/decay slice of what teams build on top.

**OSS license posture.** Apache-2.0: Flink, RisingWave (the explicit Apache-2.0 challenger to Materialize), Spark Structured Streaming (Apache, but Real-Time Mode is Databricks-runtime-exclusive in practice), Kafka Streams, ksqlDB (Confluent Community License — source-available, not OSI-OSS). BSL: Materialize 1.1. Proprietary/managed-only: Confluent Cloud Flink, AWS MSF, Databricks Real-Time Mode. Beava's Apache-2.0 is a real wedge against Materialize specifically.

**Hyperscaler bundling threat.** High. Confluent Cloud for Apache Flink unifies Kafka+Flink+Schema Registry+Connect; AWS MSK + MSF; Databricks Real-Time Mode (sub-300 ms p99 inside Spark runtime customers already pay for); Snowflake Snowpipe Streaming + Dynamic Tables; Microsoft Fabric Real-Time Intelligence. A buyer with a hyperscaler relationship rarely evaluates an independent engine in 2025-2026 unless the use case is sub-ms p99, single-binary edge, tens-of-MB RAM, no JVM — exactly beava's niche.

**Integration surface.** Kafka, Pulsar, Kinesis, Redpanda, AWS-IoT, Azure-IoT, MQTT as upstream producers; CDC (Debezium). Flink ships hundreds of source/sink connectors. Sinks: Kafka topics, JDBC, Iceberg/Delta Lake, ClickHouse, Pinot. Beava's HTTP/JSON + framed-TCP+msgpack push surface is fundamentally different — it expects the producer to push events to beava (matching SDK collectors like Segment/RudderStack/Snowplow/PostHog/Mixpanel, and AWS IoT Rules / Azure IoT Hub egress patterns), not for beava to consume a Kafka topic. Fit for SDK-first behavioral analytics (use case 5) and AWS-IoT-Rules-style IoT egress (13); friction for any buyer already standardized on Kafka.

**Scoring engine coupling.** Stream engines couple to rule engines (Drools, OPA, Flink CEP itself for in-engine pattern matching) and ML scorers. Materialize and RisingWave often serve query results directly via Postgres protocol. Beava lives in the data path as a feature backend, never the decisioning path.

### Beava position lens

**Position today.** demo

**Credible wedge.** (1) Apache-2.0 with no open-core rug and no BSL gate. (2) Single binary, no JVM, no Kafka prerequisite — beava boots in milliseconds at tens of MB of RAM where a baseline Flink job demands JobManager + TaskManagers + state-backend + checkpoint sink. (3) Sub-ms TCP+msgpack get/batch_get for the serve side — Flink/Spark are pipeline engines that write to an external store; beava IS the store. (4) ~60 pre-built aggregation primitives via Python SDK — no Flink SQL, Java DataStream, Scala, or Materialize PostgreSQL DDL. (5) Push-on-event HTTP/TCP ingress matches the SDK-first behavioral-analytics shape (5) and AWS-IoT-Rules/Azure-IoT-Hub egress shape (13) far better than Kafka-first engines. (6) The "streaming MVs converging with OLAP" trend (RisingWave + Materialize + Spark RTM all pitching MV-as-serving-store) validates the architectural seam — except beava skips the SQL surface and ships pre-aggregated features directly. Honest framing: beava is not a Flink competitor; it is the per-entity-rolling-counter slice of what teams build on top of Flink+Redis, collapsed into one binary.

**Disqualifiers.** (1) No general stream-processing — no windowed joins, CEP, complex event patterns, cross-stream enrichment, arbitrary SQL transforms. Buyers who need Flink's DataStream API cannot replace Flink with beava. (2) No Kafka/Kinesis/Pulsar consumer in v0 — every published Flink production reference assumes Kafka is the source of truth. (3) No exactly-once semantics — Flink's checkpointing + 2PC sinks, Spark's idempotent micro-batch + Delta, RisingWave's barrier-based checkpoints all guarantee exactly-once; beava is at-least-once at best. (4) No event-time processing in v0 — beava is processing-time-only and rejects `event_time`/`event_time_ms` fields explicitly (`unknown_field_event_time_v0`), disqualifying any use case where late-arriving events must be windowed correctly. (5) No TLS, no auth. (6) No in-process sharding — single beava node caps around 684k EPS / tens of GB hot state, where Flink/Spark/RisingWave scale horizontally to millions of EPS. (7) No SQL surface — Materialize, RisingWave, Flink SQL, ksqlDB all let analysts write `CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW`. (8) v0 events-only / no `kind: table` register, no joins, no sessions, no triggers — explicitly out of scope per locked invariants.

**Time to credible offering.** 6-12mo. Kafka/Kinesis/MQTT consumer bridge, TLS+auth, event-time+watermarking, horizontal sharding for >684k EPS or >50 GB hot-state tenants, at-least-once-with-idempotency-key. None individually is a multi-quarter job, but together they are a 6-12 month roadmap.

### Evidence and verdict

**Verdict.** **Watch** (do not directly compete on general stream-processing). Beava is structurally not a Flink/Spark/Materialize/RisingWave replacement — it covers the count/mean/quantile/decay slice that teams build on top, collapsed into one binary. Position beava as the "feature serving tier you put behind Flink (or instead of Flink + Redis) for per-entity rolling counters", not as a streaming-engine alternative. Monitor RisingWave specifically — closest architectural cousin (Rust, Apache-2.0, single-binary-capable, MV-as-serving-store) and the engine most likely to expand into beava's territory by adding push-on-event ingress or a Postgres-protocol get-by-entity-key surface. Avoid framing battles where the buyer needs event-time + watermarks, exactly-once, or arbitrary SQL.

**Confidence.** high

## H3 — Real-time KV / in-memory state

**Use-case refs:** 3, 12, 14b, 18a, 18b

### Competitive landscape

**Incumbents.** The densest and most-contested beava-adjacent market. (A) **Valkey** — BSD-3-clause Linux-Foundation fork of Redis 7.2.4, became cloud-default in 2025-2026 (AWS ElastiCache for Valkey is default, GCP Memorystore for Valkey ships first-class, Ubuntu 26.04 ships Valkey as the `redis` package). Valkey 8.1 publishes ~8% higher ops/sec, 22% lower p99, 20% lower memory than Redis OSS 7.2. (B) **Redis 8** — relicensed May 2025 under tri-license (AGPLv3 + RSALv2 + SSPLv1) after the March 2024 SSPL/RSALv2 controversy. Redis 8 GA folded JSON, time-series, the five probabilistic structures (Bloom, Cuckoo, CMS, Top-K, t-digest) and Vector Sets (HNSW, beta) into core, but **RedisGears triggers/functions (TFCALL/TFUNCTION) were deprecated and will be removed.** (C) **Dragonfly** — multi-threaded shared-nothing Redis-protocol server, ~2.4x throughput vs Valkey on 16 vCPUs / ~4.5x on 48 vCPUs, 38% less memory; BSL-licensed. (D) **Aerospike** — high-end commercial flash+RAM hybrid KV owning tier-1 AdTech and fraud feature-store accounts (Trade Desk, Criteo, PayPal, Barclays, LexisNexis, Sony Interactive Entertainment, Quantcast, Adobe, Airtel, HDFC, Grab, TransUnion, Wayfair); proprietary commercial. (E) Hazelcast (Apache-2.0 community + Enterprise commercial), RonDB (GPLv2, exclusive Hopsworks backing, ~100M lookups/sec via REST), KeyDB (under Snap).

**OSS license posture.** Hardened around permissive-or-fork posture in 2025-2026. Valkey: BSD-3, LF governance. Redis 8: tri-licensed AGPLv3 + RSALv2 + SSPLv1 — AGPLv3 is OSI-approved but copyleft scope blocks many enterprise procurement filters, and the 2024 SSPL/RSALv2 "rug-pull" burned community trust. Dragonfly: BSL. Aerospike: proprietary commercial (AGPL Community). Hazelcast: Apache-2.0 community + Enterprise. RonDB: GPLv2. Beava: Apache-2.0 — strictly more permissive than every named incumbent except Valkey. License posture is now a hard buyer filter, not a tiebreaker.

**Hyperscaler bundling threat.** **High** — the single highest-bundling-threat segment in the beava use-case map. Every hyperscaler ships a managed in-memory KV at a price-point a single-binary OSS challenger must beat: AWS ElastiCache for Valkey (20% cheaper than Redis SKU), AWS MemoryDB for Valkey (durable variant), GCP Memorystore for Valkey ($0.034/GB-hour Standard vs $0.0425 Redis), Azure Cache for Redis. **Redis Feature Form** (launched April 2026 preview) is the explicit bundling threat against Beava in the feature-store role.

**Integration surface.** Buyers expect Kafka/Kinesis/Pulsar producers (default for Feast/Tecton/Hopsworks/Chalk/Fennel/OpenMeter/Lago, baked into AWS RTB Fabric reference architectures); direct HTTP webhook/mobile-SDK push (adtech win-notices, billing meter events, marketplace clickstreams); framed TCP+msgpack (beava's novel sub-ms edge). Beava does NOT ship a native Kafka consumer in v0 — a sidecar pattern is the standard workaround across the use cases, and the absence is the most frequently-cited gap.

**Scoring engine coupling.** Both, by design — KV is the substrate. Frequency capping (3) and quota gates (14b) are deterministic rules read directly from the KV. Pricing (12), fraud scoring (18a), recsys ranking (18b) layer an ML scorer above the KV serving 20-500 features per decision via batched reads.

### Beava position lens

**Position today.** demo

**Credible wedge.** (1) Sub-ms TCP+msgpack get/batch_get vs Redis-Lua 1-5ms and Vertex AI FS ~30ms — beava's wire format and threading model put it in Aerospike/Dragonfly latency territory; 684k EPS sustained on a single Apple-M4 core. (2) **~50-60 first-class aggregation primitives baked into the server vs Redis/Valkey requiring Lua scripts or RedisGears (the latter deprecated in 2025) or RedisTimeSeries downsampling** — single most defensible differentiator against the Redis/Valkey-as-feature-store pattern. (3) Single binary, no Kafka/Flink/Redis-cluster dependency. (4) Apache-2.0 with no open-core split and no SSPL/BSL trap. (5) WAL+snapshot per-entity durability with refuse-on-network-FS invariant — Redis AOF is per-instance and cannot give the "fsync this counter, not the whole store" semantic that billing metering (14b) and exact fraud counters (18a) need.

### Evidence and verdict

**Verdict.** **Compete** — beava has a genuine wedge (declarative aggregation primitives + single binary + sub-ms TCP + Apache-2.0) against the Redis+Lua DIY pattern that dominates mid-market AdTech, fraud, recsys, and billing-metering, but tier-1 wins (Trade Desk-class AdTech, PayPal-class fraud, DoorDash-class recsys, global metering) require closing the TLS/auth + sharding + replication + idempotency-key gaps over 6-12 months. **Lead-tier positioning** is possible against (Redis+Lua) and (Lago/OpenMeter ClickHouse) in mid-market accounts; Compete-tier against (Tecton/Hopsworks/Chalk/Fennel managed feature stores); Watch-tier against (Aerospike at tier-1 scale, Redis Feature Form once GA, hyperscaler bundles).

**Confidence.** high

## H4 — Streaming OLAP / "real-time enough"

**Use-case refs:** 5, 14

### Competitive landscape

**Incumbents.** ClickHouse (Apache-2.0, ClickHouse Inc. with Cloud open-core), Apache Pinot (Apache-2.0, ASF, commercialized by StarTree — $75M total funding, $47M Series B 2022), Apache Druid (Apache-2.0, ASF, commercialized by Imply), StarRocks (Apache-2.0, Linux Foundation, commercialized by CelerData), Apache Doris, Tinybird (proprietary on top of ClickHouse — $30M Series B June 2024 at $240M valuation), Hydrolix ($80M Series C April 2025, streaming data lake; >650 customers, 8x revenue growth in 2024), Imply Polaris (managed Druid), StarTree Cloud (managed Pinot), CelerData (managed StarRocks). All target sub-second ad-hoc analytical SQL on freshly-ingested event streams. The category is structurally an ad-hoc-SQL-on-streams play, not a per-entity feature server.

**OSS license posture.** Mixed. Open-source engines (ClickHouse core, Pinot, Druid, StarRocks, Doris) are Apache-2.0 — same as beava. ClickHouse Inc. shows open-core drift: SharedMergeTree (cloud-native storage-compute separation) and lightweight UPDATE are ClickHouse Cloud-only. Pinot and Druid are pure ASF projects (vendor-neutral). StarRocks is Linux Foundation (vendor-neutral). Tinybird is proprietary. Hydrolix is proprietary. The OSS engines are license-clean and not a competitive license wedge for beava.

**Hyperscaler bundling threat.** **High.** Snowflake Dynamic Tables (1-minute minimum target lag, GA 2024), BigQuery streaming + materialized views, Databricks Real-Time Mode, AWS Redshift Streaming Ingestion now cover the "real-time enough" (1-60s freshness) regime. ClickHouse Cloud is itself a hyperscaler-marketplace play. Confluent + Flink + Snowflake or Confluent + Tinybird is a one-line marketplace add-on. A buyer evaluating "do I need real-time analytics?" will reach for a marketplace bundle 80%+ of the time.

**Integration surface.** Kafka-first by default. Pinot ingests from Kafka, Pulsar, Kinesis. Druid has native Kafka/Kinesis with query-on-arrival at millions of events/sec. ClickHouse has Kafka table engine + Materialized Views as the canonical streaming-ingest pattern; Tinybird ships Kafka/Confluent connectors. The buyer expectation: "point a Kafka topic at it and write SQL". Beava's HTTP-push/TCP+msgpack model is a friction for streaming-OLAP buyers but fits the OTel-collector→HTTP exporter and Envoy access-log tail paths cleanly.

**Scoring engine coupling.** Mostly external. Streaming OLAP is read-by-dashboards and read-by-alerters, not by inline ML scorers. Buyers run Grafana/Metabase/Superset/in-house React dashboards on top. Inline rules engines are rare; inline ML scorers are essentially absent — the latency profile (10ms-1s queries) is wrong for inline gating. This is exactly where beava is structurally different.

### Beava position lens

**Position today.** demo

**Credible wedge.** Beava and streaming OLAP serve different verbs (per-entity get vs ad-hoc aggregate SQL) at different latency budgets (sub-ms point lookup at 10k-100k+ QPS vs 10ms-1s ad-hoc aggregate at 50-500 QPS concurrency). **Public ClickHouse benchmarks state ~4K lookups/sec on MergeTree tables and ~10K via Dictionary tables — and the ClickHouse docs themselves say "not suitable for a high load of simple point queries"** because each key forces a granule scan and column decompression. Pinot/Druid have the same shape. That gap is beava's credible wedge for the in-path feature-serving role: a single beava binary serves the rolling per-(user_id, route, tenant_id) feature vector to a real-time decision in <1ms, while the OLAP backend remains the analyst surface alongside. Apache-2.0 with no open-core (vs ClickHouse Cloud's SharedMergeTree split) is secondary. Single binary with WAL+snapshot (no Kafka, no Flink, no Kubernetes-cluster-of-coordinators-and-historicals as Druid requires) is the third wedge.

**Disqualifiers.** Beava cannot replace a streaming OLAP system in its actual job: ad-hoc SQL exploration, funnels, cohorts, paths, joins across tenant × route × time × user, retroactive event redefinition (Heap-style), high-concurrency dashboards with 50-500 dimensions selectable. v0 has no SQL surface, no joins, no cross-entity aggregation at query time, no ad-hoc filtering — features are precomputed declaratively. Beava cannot serve a 28-day SLO compliance roll-up (in-memory only, no long-term tier). No Kafka native ingest, no Iceberg/Parquet sink, no Grafana data-source plugin, no Prometheus remote-write receiver, no OTel-collector exporter — every one is table-stakes for an OLAP buyer. No TLS/auth, no in-process sharding above ~684k EPS / ~50GB hot state per node, no multi-tenancy. **The buyer mismatch is the dominant story:** OLAP buyer is analyst/SRE asking "let me write SQL on fresh events"; beava buyer is risk/personalization/platform asking "serve me this feature on the next request in <1ms".

**Time to credible offering.** 12mo+ — and only if the goal is "compete in this segment." For "coexist as the hot per-entity tier in front of an OLAP backend" the time is 3-6mo (TLS/auth + OTel exporter + Kafka consumer + Parquet/Iceberg sink + documented OLAP-coexistence reference architecture). Amazon Neptune is now bundled into AWS spend with the October 2025 GraphStorm integration making "train a GNN with GraphStorm + serve subgraph-neighborhood inference against Neptune Database" a few-clicks deployment; AWS Entity Resolution + Neptune Analytics is a published fraud-detection reference architecture. Google Cloud Vertex AI + Neo4j AuraDS is a co-marketed bundled offering. Azure Cosmos DB for Apache Gremlin is bundled into Azure subscriptions but lags on fraud-vertical marketing. **Integration surface.** Heterogeneous, dominated by Kafka and HTTP. Upstream into the graph DB: Kafka is universal at the production-fraud scale (Neo4j Streams / Neo4j Connector for Kafka is the canonical pattern; TigerGraph Kafka loader; Memgraph + Pulsar/Kafka streams; Neptune + Kinesis/MSK), with batch CSV/Parquet loaders for backfill. **Scoring engine coupling.** Both, with strong separation between the data path and the decisioning path. **Position today.** none

(5) Push-on-event HTTP/JSON + framed-TCP avoids the Kafka requirement that Tecton/Feast/Hopsworks assume. **The framing for outbound pitches is explicit: "beava + Memgraph (or TigerGraph or Neo4j) together replace the Redis+Lua+Neo4j stitched stack", not "beava replaces a graph DB".**

Within the windowed-aggregation half: (1) No TLS/auth on :8080/:8081/:8090 in v0 blocks any procurement-conscious bank/PSP/marketplace buyer. (2) No in-process sharding and ~684k EPS single-core / ~100-200 GB hot-state ceiling means beava is a per-region or per-tenant slice, not a global feature backend. (3) No cross-region replication. (4) In-memory only with refuse-on-network-FS posture. (5) **No triggers / no "fire when counter crosses threshold"** — particularly painful in the graph-adjacent slot because the production pattern is "feature crosses threshold → trigger k-hop graph query for ring detection → feed cluster label back as a feature", and today beava cannot initiate that. (6) No multi-tenancy/namespace ACLs blocks the device-intel-vendor-hosted-tenant pattern. (8) No managed/hosted offering, no published integration runbook for beava + Neo4j or beava + Memgraph or beava + Neptune — first piece of documentation a design-partner would ask for.
